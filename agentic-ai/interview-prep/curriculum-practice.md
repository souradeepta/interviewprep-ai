# Agentic AI Curriculum Practice

Use each scenario as a short interview drill. State assumptions, propose a concrete approach, define how you would evaluate it, and identify a failure mode.

## What Is an Agent?

### Prompt
Design a small system that applies what is an agent? to a support workflow that must answer account questions while avoiding unauthorized changes. Specify the inputs, decision boundary, success metric, resource limit, and one case where the system must stop and ask for human review.

### Role/Domain
Agentic AI engineering; present the design and operational plan to a senior interviewer.

### Difficulty
Intermediate

### Expected Answer
A strong response explains the mechanism of what is an agent?, states assumptions about available data and authority, and gives a concrete baseline. It defines success as correct task completion under constraints, proposes an evaluation set with ordinary and adversarial cases, and measures quality, p95 latency, and cost per completed task. The design validates inputs and outputs, limits retries or actions, and makes failure visible through useful traces. It identifies when the method should be disabled or escalated.

### Evaluation
Score mechanism accuracy, explicit assumptions, baseline comparison, measurable quality and operational metrics, bounded failure handling, and a clear human escalation rule. Ask the candidate to explain how each metric would change the design decision.

### Follow-up
A release increases completion rate but also doubles p95 latency and creates one unauthorized action in a red-team case. How would you debug the regression, decide whether to roll back, and update the evaluation set?

### Common Mistake
Describing the technique in isolation without defining the authority boundary, a baseline, or evidence that the workflow is safe and useful.
